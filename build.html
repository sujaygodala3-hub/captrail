02 · Check first

Measurements to check

Check these before you start: the stand’s overall size isn’t the same as the working clearance under the lens.

MeasurementWhat to do
Cradle and base board sizeRead the imported STL dimensions in the slicer
Finger channel width and depthCheck that the printed groove comfortably fits your finger
Lens-to-finger clearanceFind experimentally while focusing
Height of nailfold above tabletopMeasure with the finger resting naturally
Working space beside the stand postMake sure the cradle fits beneath the lens
Calibration card heightImage the card at about the same height as the nailfold

04 · Assembly

Step by step

  1. 01

    Print the cradle and base board

    Print both parts in PLA, lying flat on the bed. Printing them standing up made them curve.

    The pegs are 4.6 mm and press into the cradle’s 6 mm holes; add a dot of glue if they’re loose.

    Print times on our Elegoo Neptune 4 Pro: base board about 2 h 40 min, cradle about 35 min, logo letters about 15 min. The cradle is based on the Attune finger rest.

  2. 02

    Set up the microscope stand

    Put the microscope in its included stand, lens pointing down. Tighten the stand so the microscope doesn’t drift down.

  3. 03

    Position the cradle

    Click the cradle onto the pegs on the base board and sit the stand in the board’s dip, so the position is the same every time.

    Keep the hand and wrist supported and make sure nothing squeezes the finger.

  4. 04

    Connect and test the microscope

    Plug the USB cable into your computer and open a camera app or our Microscope page. Check that the live image appears.

  5. 05

    Adjust focus and lighting

    Turn the LED down low; bright light causes glare.

    Focus with the focus wheel only, and don’t touch it again after calibrating.

  6. 06

    Test stability

    Lift and reposition your finger a few times. Because the cradle locks onto the pegs, it should land in the same place each time.

  7. 07

    Calibrate measurements

    Put the calibration card under the lens and focus on its coordinate cross (0.1 mm per small tick, 1 mm per long line).

    On the Microscope page, click the outer edges of two long lines that are 2 mm apart and enter 2000 µm:

    µm per pixel = known distance in µmdistance in pixels

    Our result: 6.094 µm per pixel at 640 × 480.

    Recalibrate if zoom or focus changes.

  8. 08

    Capture your first nailfold image

    Put your finger back in the cradle. Use a drop of mineral oil on the nailfold only, no plastic wrap, and avoid irritated or broken skin.

    Keep any glare on the nail, not the skin.

    Use Best of 8 and keep only sharp frames.

    Save images with Enhance off for the dataset.

Bottom line

The open question isn’t the assembly. It’s whether this microscope can take clear, repeatable nailfold images good enough for the AI. Test that before making the hardware more complicated.
