02 · Check first

Measurements to check

The stand’s listed dimensions are overall size, not necessarily the footprint or working clearance under the lens.

MeasurementWhat to do
Finger rest length, width, heightRead the imported STL dimensions in the slicer
Finger channel width and depthCheck that the printed groove comfortably fits your finger
Lens-to-finger clearanceFind experimentally while focusing
Height of nailfold above tabletopMeasure with the finger resting naturally
Working space beside the stand postMake sure the finger rest fits beneath the lens
Calibration slide thicknessUse the spec or measure it; it matters for matching imaging height

04 · Assembly

Step by step

  1. 01

    Prepare the 3D print

    Download the Attune finger-rest STL and open it in your slicer.

    Set scale to 100%, read its real dimensions, check the print orientation and print one rest. Smooth any edges that touch skin.

  2. 02

    Assemble the Celestron stand

    Attach the vertical support to its base and fit the microscope into the holder following Celestron’s instructions. Tighten the controls so the microscope doesn’t drift down.

  3. 03

    Position the finger rest

    Put the setup directly on your desk and slide the finger rest under the lens. Place the nailfold under the lens.

    Keep the hand and wrist supported and make sure nothing squeezes the finger.

  4. 04

    Connect and test the microscope

    Plug the USB cable into your computer and open the Celestron software, a camera app, or our Microscope page. Check that the live image appears.

  5. 05

    Adjust focus and lighting

    Raise or lower the microscope on the stand, then fine-tune with the focus wheel.

    Start at moderate LED brightness. If your version has a polarizing filter, turn it to cut glare.

  6. 06

    Test stability

    Lift and reposition your finger a few times. If the rest moves, add thin double-sided tape under it. Don’t attach anything permanently until you’ve found a comfortable position.

  7. 07

    Calibrate measurements

    Move your finger aside and put the calibration slide under the lens on a clean, flat support, at about the same height as the nailfold. Capture a sharp image of the markings and work out the scale:

    µm per pixel = known distance in µmdistance in pixels

    Example: if a 100 µm interval spans 200 pixels, the scale is 0.5 µm per pixel. The Microscope page can do this for you.

    Keep settings and geometry the same. Recalibrate if magnification, resolution or working distance changes.

  8. 08

    Capture your first nailfold image

    Remove the slide and put your finger back in the rest. Add a small drop of mineral oil to the nailfold, avoiding irritated or broken skin.

    Recheck focus and lighting, then save several sharp images with the same settings.

    If you can’t see the capillary loops, adjust finger angle, focus and lighting before buying more hardware.

Bottom line

The open question isn’t the assembly. It’s whether this microscope can take clear, repeatable nailfold images good enough for the AI. Test that before making the hardware more complicated.
